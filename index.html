Uncertainty ellipses

The reported 50 % confidence ellipse: semi-major and semi-minor axis in kilometres, rotated to the reported angle. Faint on purpose so a hundred of them stacked on one storm cell still show where the network was sure and where it was guessing.

Peak current

Absolute value of the peak current in kA. Click a class to hide it, or type a range above. Negative strokes are the ordinary kind; positive ones are rarer, often stronger, and carry more charge.

Peak current is an estimate from the sensor network, easily 20 % out on any single stroke, and it is not a danger threshold. Every cloud-to-ground stroke in this file is thousands of times more current than a heart can take. What current changes is how far the hazard reaches from the attachment point, not whether there is one.

Altitude

——

Unfixed, the ramp stretches to whatever is on screen: the lowest ground in view is dark blue, the highest is pale. Pan into a valley and it restretches, so colours mean different heights in different views. Fix the scale to compare one place against another.

Blues and greens throughout, so the warm strike colours stay readable on top. Sea is left clear. Heights come from open 10–30 m elevation tiles, computed in your browser.

Lightning data © Meteorologisk institutt, distributed under NLOD / CC BY 4.0, detected by the Nordic lightning network. Positions are triangulated, not observed: a point can be a kilometre or more from where the flash actually hit, which is what the ellipse is telling you. Detection efficiency is lower for cloud-to-cloud than for cloud-to-ground, and lower everywhere far offshore.
